Why redirect chains matter

A redirect chain occurs when one URL redirects to another URL that redirects again before reaching the final destination. Redirects are normal, but unnecessary hops add latency and make website migrations, canonicalization and debugging harder.

What to look for

Check that the final destination is the page you expect, that HTTP-to-HTTPS and hostname normalization are consistent, and that old URLs do not bounce through several historical locations. Redirect loops should be fixed immediately because clients never reach usable content.

SEO and migration use cases

During a domain or URL migration, direct old URLs to the closest relevant new destination. After launch, update internal links so visitors and crawlers do not need to traverse redirects for normal navigation.

Frequently asked questions

How many redirect hops are ideal?

The best chain is usually the shortest one that correctly implements your routing goal. Avoid adding hops that provide no functional value.

Can redirects change by user?

Yes. Cookies, device detection, geolocation, authentication and CDN rules can produce different redirect behavior for different clients.
