    What the crawler checks

    Search-engine fundamentals before scores and buzzwords

    The audit separates observable technical evidence from editorial heuristics. It does not invent rankings, traffic or AI citations.

    Crawl & indexability

    HTTP status, redirect paths, robots.txt, Googlebot/Bingbot access, XML sitemap discovery, meta robots and X-Robots-Tag.

    Canonicalization

    Canonical presence, cross-host canonicals, HTTPS destination and conflicting sitemap/noindex signals.

    On-page SEO

    Titles, meta descriptions, primary headings, duplicate metadata, readable text and basic document semantics.

    Site architecture

    Internal-link discovery, crawl depth, redirecting links, broken crawled URLs and sitemap pages with no discovered inbound links.

    Structured data

    JSON-LD presence and syntax validity. Supported rich-result eligibility still depends on Google's feature-specific requirements.

    SXO & mobile

    Responsive viewport, crawler response latency, navigation paths and user-facing issues that can make a site harder to use.

    GEO / AIO readiness

    Indexable textual content, internal discoverability, valid structured data and trust/identity signals. No special “AI schema” score is fabricated.

    Bing discovery

    Bingbot access and sitemap health are checked publicly. IndexNow and Bing AI citation performance require site-owner workflows and cannot be inferred reliably from a crawl.

    What “Google and Bing compliant” can—and cannot—mean

    Search engines publish technical requirements and best practices, but passing an audit does not guarantee crawling, indexing, rankings, rich results or AI citations. This tool checks public signals that can be observed from outside the site and explains conflicts that are likely to deserve attention.

    Google's current guidance for AI Overviews and AI Mode says there are no additional technical requirements beyond normal Search eligibility: pages need to be indexable and eligible to appear with a snippet, while standard SEO fundamentals such as crawl access, internal links, textual content, page experience and accurate structured data continue to matter. That is why the GEO/AIO section reuses verifiable search fundamentals instead of assigning a fictional AI score.

    How severity is assigned

    Critical

    Signals that can directly prevent or seriously conflict with discovery/indexability, such as crawl blocks, broken URLs or noindex on sitemap pages.

    Warning

    Important technical or content issues that can weaken crawl efficiency, snippets, canonicalization, architecture or mobile usability.

    Notice

    Heuristics and quality opportunities that deserve review but are not treated as fixed Google/Bing ranking requirements.

    Passed

    Checks where the crawl found positive evidence. A pass only applies to the tested signal and crawl scope.

    Current crawl limits

    Each free run audits up to 50 same-host pages within a bounded execution window. It seeds discovery from the final landing URL, internal links and available XML sitemap URLs. Large websites can therefore exceed the current free crawl scope. JavaScript rendering, private Search Console/Bing Webmaster data and CrUX field metrics are not part of this first public version.
